B1: please add to the DESIGN.md Decisions log: pessimistic, conditional and redis take pg_advisory_xact_lock(user, event) first, so one buyer's concurrent POST /api/holds requests take turns and the buyer keeps at most one active hold per event (§7.1); without it 10 parallel requests left 10 active holds (race.test.ts).
B1: please add to the DESIGN.md Decisions log: the holds rate limit runs as route middleware right after requireAuth, before the other §7.1 pre-checks (the spec lists it last), so a throttled client costs no database reads.
B1: S1's createPool hard-codes connectionTimeoutMillis 5000, and pg-pool applies it to queued requests. Under heavy machine load the 200-way pessimistic race test once took 4.65 s (normally 0.5-1 s); under k6, pessimistic (which holds a connection while it waits on row locks) will turn pool waits over 5 s into hold errors. Consider a PG_CONNECT_TIMEOUT_MS config.

B1: FYI W1: with HOLD_STRATEGY=redis, re-holding a seat that is in your own active hold is answered SEATS_UNAVAILABLE until that hold is released (DELETE /api/holds/:id), because the Lua gate (ARGV = holdId, ttlMs) can't tell your hold from someone else's. conditional and pessimistic handle it.
